Point-of-Impact Discrepancy — Reconstruction vs. Physical Evidence

Geotab's Collision Reconstruction depicts the vehicle receiving a passenger-side impact. Scene evidence shows a different configuration: frontal. The reconstruction's estimate derives from accelerometer values present at its 08:22:16.000 trigger timestamp (-0.23 g longitudinal / -2.23 g lateral, the ft/s² figures printed in the report) — approximately 0.9 seconds after the recorded peak, when the longitudinal signal had already decayed; the residual sample reads to the algorithm as a broadside load. The full high-rate capture shows a single pulse at 08:22:15.120–08:22:15.410 with peaks of -2.19 g longitudinal / -2.23 g lateral / +2.42 g vertical. Near-equal longitudinal and lateral peaks with a pronounced vertical excursion — a tri-axial pulse. This pattern arises from terrain traversal or from a frontal strike into a crossing vehicle with front-end override of the struck vehicle's structure; scene evidence decides which. Geotab's own report notes the point of impact is an accelerometer-derived estimate that may vary; its impact graphic should not be relied upon for this event.
